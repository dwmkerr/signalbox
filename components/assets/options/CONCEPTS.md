# Hero concept bank (from the hero-explore workflow, 2026-07-23)

Inspiration only - kept per request. 6 of 12 concept agents completed (others dropped connections). Not shipping assets; a menu of directions to draw the one hero + supporting shots from.

## Focus Pull  ()
**Concept:** A rack-focus reveal: your agent-running desktop goes soft and dim while the signalbox jumplist snaps into focus over it like Spotlight for your agents, caret already blinking, the amber ask that needs you already selected.
**Hook:** A dev who runs many agents recognises the exact gesture from Spotlight/Raycast/Linear - so it reads instantly as "an app-switcher for my agents" before a word is read - and the frosted backdrop says "this lives over your actual work"; then the caret blinking in a real search field and a query filtering live to the agent that needs you proves it's a tool you drive, not a screenshot you admire.
**Differs:** vs the split chaos→calm hero: that stages SHARP, rotated terminal windows BESIDE the board as a jittering "before" pile and runs on sore-fingers/relief anxiety copy. Focus Pull puts an ordinary desktop BEHIND the palette, frosted and dimmed - a rack-focus overlay, not a side-by-side comparison - and the emotion is calm focus, not relief. No chaos pile, no pain-point headline.
vs the plain board (demo.html): that floats the board on an abstract radial gradient with NOTHING behind it and simply holds after one keycap press. Focus Pull supplies the missing context - your real blurred workspace behind the glass - and, crucially, USES the search: a type-to-filter beat that neither existing asset does, reframing the product from "the board appears" to "spotlight for your agents."
Both existing heroes share the "summon the board" spine; Focus Pull differentiates on that spine two ways - the reveal is a cinematographic focus-pull over a frosted real backdrop (a look neither has), and the payoff is the palette being operated (typed query, live filter, `#tag`-style triage), not merely displayed.
**Agent screens behind:** True  ·  **Mobile:** False
**Refs:** macOS Spotlight - dimmed desktop with a single focused field floating high over blurred wallpaper; Raycast - dark keyboard-first command palette with a list-left / detail-right layout and ⌘-space summon; Linear command menu (⌘K) - frosted overlay, dense dark rows, keyboard-driven; Superhuman command palette - keyboard-first inbox triage, apt because the board is an inbox for agents; Rack focus / focus-pull cinematography - background racks soft as the subject snaps sharp, the visual metaphor the name borrows

## The Lit Row  ()
**Concept:** A single, already-open board sitting in a dark room with every working agent dimmed to a hum and the one agent blocked on a decision lit amber, its real question readable from across the room, so the board triages for you before you have read a word.
**Hook:** It looks like a photo of your own screen at 11pm - a dark board full of agents grinding away - except one line glows amber and you can read it from across the room: a real question about your live worker config, not "needs permission". Before you have read anything else, your eye has already found the one that needs you. A dev running six worktrees recognizes that exact "oh, that one" and stops.
**Differs:** The split hero stages chaos-vs-calm across a divider and its whole spine is press ⌃⌥J -> board materializes -> select amber -> jump lands in the pile; every beat is acted-out navigation. This has no divider, no chaos pile, no before/after, no summon beat, no jump-landing - one board, already open, and the keycap is a static label, not the animated climax. Versus the plain board (which lights every row equally so you scan it yourself), this dims six rows into shadow and lights exactly one, turning the board from a list you read into a spotlight that reads for you. It also differs from the faithful-still idea by putting nothing behind the board - pure dark - internalizing everything-else as the dimmed rows rather than as blurred terminals stacked behind.
**Agent screens behind:** False  ·  **Mobile:** False
**Refs:** macOS Spotlight - the literal metaphor: one lit result surfacing out of the dark; Raycast / Alfred command-palette heroes - the jumplist genre, shot with the top result emphasized; Linear's dark, hairline, dense-row board aesthetic (the 'Linear look' the brief names) - near-black ground, one rare accent; Superhuman's one-thing-at-a-time / triage framing inherited from the email-inbox lineage the board borrows; Chiaroscuro product photography (iA Writer, Teenage Engineering) - a single element lit warm in an otherwise unlit dark frame

## The Beacon (Top-Right)  ()
**Concept:** Crop to the top-right corner of a Mac: the signalbox beacon in the menu bar wearing one amber status dot, with its native light-appearance dropdown hanging open below it — the product's whole pitch is that it already lives up here and the corner tells you when an agent needs you, before you go looking.
**Hook:** A dev who runs seven agents recognizes this exact pixel real estate — it's their own menu bar — and feels the specific anxiety it resolves: "is one of them blocked on me right now?" The answer isn't a click or a search; it's a glance at the corner. One amber dot, top-right, means yes, and the dropdown names which one. It sells the thing the other heroes can't: signalbox isn't an app you open, it's an always-on peripheral vision for your agents. The passive "the corner already knows" beat is more relaxing than any "summon the board" demo — you don't even have to act to get the signal.
**Differs:** Both existing heroes are all-dark and both pivot on the same beat — press ⌃⌥J and the board materializes. This one never summons: it leads with the passive, ambient corner. Three concrete departures. (1) It shows the macOS menu bar and the single urgency-ranked status dot — the ambient-glance mechanic — which neither the split nor the plain board depicts at all. (2) It renders the NATIVE LIGHT-APPEARANCE dropdown (the #f5f5f7 menu from menubar.html), a surface both existing dark assets omit entirely; the light native menu over a dark desktop is instantly, unmistakably "macOS menu bar app." (3) The emotional spine is "the corner already tells you," not chaos-vs-calm (split) and not "here is the board" (plain board); ⌃⌥J is demoted from the pivot to a secondary depth cue, and the click-path — the entry point neither existing hero shows — leads.
**Agent screens behind:** False  ·  **Mobile:** False
**Refs:** macOS status-bar-app heroes that ARE the menu-bar dropdown: Bartender, Ice, iStat Menus, CleanShot X, Dropover, Meeter, Fantastical's menu-bar item — all shoot the native light dropdown hanging from the corner as the hero; Raycast / Alfred for the ⌃⌥J keycap grammar — borrowed but deliberately demoted, since this concept leads with passive presence rather than the summon; Linear's dark, dense, mono-accented panel for the secondary dark jump-box glimpse; Apple's own macOS marketing convention of a translucent menu bar over a wallpaper gradient to signal 'top of your screen'

## Roll Call  ()
**Concept:** A single board-forward jumplist where every row wears a host chip and a slim "connected machines" rail sits under the header, so the hero's whole story is your laptop, your studio mac, and your headless devbox reporting their live agent sessions into one list — and the one that needs you turns out to be on a machine you weren't even sitting at.
**Hook:** A dev running agents in git worktrees across a laptop, a desktop, and a headless server lives with a specific dread: an agent blocked on a question on a machine they're not looking at, burning an hour unseen. Roll Call shows both amber asks landing on remote hosts ([studio], [devbox]) — the pre-verbal read is "two of the things that need me are on boxes I wasn't even watching, and now I can see them in one list." That's a fear this audience already has and no tab-hunting fixes.
**Differs:** The current hero is a chaos->calm SPLIT (a jittering pile of terminal windows on the left, tidy board on the right) whose spine is press-⌃⌥J->board-materializes and whose climax is single-amber->select->jump, over sore-fingers/relief copy. The plain board draft (demo.html) is that same board held after a keycap press. Roll Call differs on all three axes the brief demands: (1) FRAMING — no split, no chaos pile, no screens behind; a single board-forward crop, and it introduces a UI element neither existing asset has: per-row host chips plus a connected-machines rail. (2) MOTION — the spine is machines reporting in and their sessions aggregating (a live roll call), not summon-the-board and not amber->jump; no jump is animated at all. (3) STORY — the emotional entry shifts from "your fingers are sore, here's relief" to "the ask was on a machine you weren't even looking at," and the ⌃⌥J keycap is recast from a press-to-reveal pivot into a static "from any machine" identity mark. It also uses fresh session data drawn from the jumplist spec (payments-webhook, codex-mcp-server, log-triage, nightly-backup), not the existing hero's fix-auth-token-expiry/Book-Prague/Fix-my-hair set, so it can't read as the old asset re-cropped.
**Agent screens behind:** False  ·  **Mobile:** True
**Refs:** Tailscale admin console / machine list — every device in one place with green online dots and hostnames; the definitive real multi-machine 'all your machines' pattern this hero's hosts rail and host chips borrow from; Linear's dark, near-black, mono-accented product surfaces — the 'Linear look' the board already targets; Raycast landing — the command palette rendered as the hero object, with the keycap chord as category identity; GitHub Actions / Vercel deployment dashboards — status rows across multiple environments and runners, the 'where it ran' column; Warp and Ghostty terminal marketing — terminal-accurate monospace authenticity as the credibility play; Superhuman / Missive inbox rows — the app-switcher/inbox row metaphor the jumplist reads as

## Full Board  ()
**Concept:** The real jump box shown at full population - one dense, centered board of thirteen live agent sessions where four amber asks glow out of a calm field of spinners, blues and rings, proving signalbox holds an entire fleet at a single glance.
**Hook:** A dev who runs six-to-ten agents across worktrees and terminals sees their own Tuesday - thirteen real sessions, spinners, a red fail, a pile of done - but resolved into ONE scannable board, and four amber asks already glowing out of the field before they read a single word. The density mirrors their chaos; the amber constellation is the promise: you will never lose the one that's asking inside the pile again. It sells scale (a whole fleet, held at once) where the other heroes sell a short tidy list.
**Differs:** vs the split hero (chaos→calm): no rotated jittering window pile, no side-by-side, no chaos→calm narrative, and no "press ⌃⌥J → board materializes" spine (the keycap is static furniture here, not the animated reveal). It is a single centered board, and the story is triage-at-scale, not messy→tidy. vs the plain board demo: ~3x the rows (13 vs 4), a fleet readout, FOUR scattered amber asks instead of one hero row, a red fail and rings so the full signal legend is present, and the glyph column reads as a six-vendor stripe. The animated beat is a live working→ask bloom (a new one lighting up mid-loop), not a keycap-reveal-then-hold. Neither existing asset conveys FLEET SCALE - that is this concept's whole job.
**Agent screens behind:** False  ·  **Mobile:** False
**Refs:** Linear inbox / issue list - dense dark rows, one accent color, the 'Linear look' the brief cites; Airport & rail departures boards - the mental model of scanning a dense status board for yours, amber as the gate call; Grafana / Datadog status dashboards - mission-control density and at-a-glance status marks (borrowing the density and glance, not the loud palette); Raycast / Alfred command palette - the jump box lineage this board descends from; Superhuman / macOS Mail - unread-bold vs read-dim rows with a right-edge age column; signalbox's own hub-jumplist.html spec (source of truth) and demo.html palette

## display-popup  ()
**Concept:** The jump box drawn as a live TUI popup — rounded box-drawing border, braille spinner, reverse-video selection — floating over your dimmed terminal scrollback and ticking in real time, like htop for your agents.
**Hook:** A dev who runs many agents pattern-matches the aesthetic in under a second: box-drawing frame, braille ⠹ spinner, colored status marks, a reverse-video selection bar — this reads as lazygit/k9s/htop, i.e. a real terminal tool a terminal person built, not a marketing mock. Then the amber row lands the gut-punch: it's an actual ask they've seen this week ("Allow: npm publish --access public?" / "overwrite the token family on reuse? (yes/no)"), and one more flips to amber live while they watch. The tell that stops the scroll is authenticity + liveness, not a headline.
**Differs:** Vs the split chaos→calm hero (hero.html): no split, no rotated/jittering pile of GUI windows, no "sore fingers / doing too much" pain copy, and no GUI board — the whole thing is one terminal in monospace box-drawing. Chaos is present but honest and minimal: one dimmed scrollback behind the popup, not a staged mess. The keycap is box-drawn, not a shadcn keycap on a divider. It proves a DIFFERENT thing — live ticking density (htop-for-agents), where a second row flips to amber while you watch — instead of the shared "step down to the one amber row, jump, done" climax. Vs the plain board (demo.html): same information, but rendered as a TUI popup over real terminal scrollback rather than a rounded GUI card floating in a radial-gradient void; braille spinner and reverse-video selection instead of macOS chrome; the preview is folded into the expanded selected row (lazygit-style) rather than absent. Vs the existing 10-terminal-native.html sketch (same angle, prior art): that one types two commands and dumps `signalbox state` as flat left-aligned scrollback text — a transcript. This one draws the jump box itself as a bordered, live, selectable TUI popup with a title counter, footer keybar, reverse-video selection, an in-row exchange preview, and an overlay-on-dimmed-scrollback composition. It's the board in a terminal, not a CLI log.
**Agent screens behind:** True  ·  **Mobile:** False
**Refs:** lazygit (multi-pane box-drawing TUI, reverse-video selection, main+detail panes); k9s (live-updating, color-coded status monitor TUI); htop / btop (persistent leave-it-open live monitor — the framing this borrows); fzf + telescope.nvim (overlay popup over dimmed scrollback with an inline preview); tmux display-popup (a centered box floating over your session — the composition and the concept name); charmbracelet gum / Bubble Tea (tasteful rounded box-drawing and restrained color); gitui (dense keyboard-driven TUI); Claude Code's own TUI (braille spinner, ✳ working glyph, ❯ prompt)
